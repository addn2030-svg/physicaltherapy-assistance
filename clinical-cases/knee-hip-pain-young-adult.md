# Clinical Case: Non-Traumatic Knee and Hip Pain in Young Adult

## Case Presentation

**Patient Profile:**
- **Age**: 20 years old
- **Gender**: Male
- **Chief Complaint**: Knee and hip pain
- **Pain Intensity**: 8/10
- **Aggravating Factors**: Stair climbing (stiffness and pain)
- **Relieving Factors**: Rest and sitting
- **Trauma History**: None reported

---

## CLINICAL REASONING FRAMEWORK

### Step 1: Comprehensive History

#### Pain Characteristics (OPQRST)
**Essential Questions:**
- **Onset**: When did pain start? Gradual or sudden? Any precipitating event?
- **Provocation**: What makes it worse? (stairs, walking, sports, sitting prolonged)
- **Palliation**: What makes it better? (rest, ice, medication, position)
- **Quality**: Sharp, dull, aching, burning, catching, locking?
- **Region/Radiation**: Exact location? Does it spread? Unilateral or bilateral?
- **Severity**: 0-10 scale at rest, with activity, at night
- **Timing**: Constant or intermittent? Morning stiffness duration? Night pain?

#### Critical History Elements

**Pain Pattern Analysis:**
- Which started first - knee or hip?
- Is pain unilateral or bilateral?
- Morning stiffness >30 minutes? (suggests inflammatory)
- Night pain or pain at rest? (RED FLAG)
- Pain with weight-bearing vs. non-weight-bearing?
- Stair pain: ascending vs. descending? (biomechanical clues)

**Functional Impact:**
- Walking distance tolerance?
- Difficulty with specific activities? (squatting, running, jumping)
- Sleep disturbance?
- Impact on work/school/sports?
- Use of assistive devices?

**Medical Screening:**
- Previous injuries to knee, hip, or lower extremity?
- Family history of arthritis or joint disease?
- Recent infections, illnesses, or systemic symptoms?
- Skin conditions (psoriasis)?
- Eye problems (uveitis)?
- GI or urogenital symptoms?
- Unexplained weight loss or fever?

**Activity/Training History:**
- Recent changes in activity level or training?
- New sports or exercise programs?
- Training errors (too much, too soon)?
- Footwear changes?
- Occupational demands?

## DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS

### Primary Considerations (High Likelihood)

#### 1. **Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (PFPS)**
**Probability**: HIGH (most common in this age group)

**Key Features:**
- Anterior knee pain, often bilateral
- Pain with stairs (especially descending - eccentric quadriceps load)
- "Movie sign" - pain with prolonged sitting
- Pain with squatting, kneeling, running
- Crepitus may be present
- Insidious onset, no trauma

**Mechanism:**
- Patellar maltracking (lateral tilt/glide)
- Increased patellofemoral joint stress
- Quadriceps weakness (especially VMO)
- Hip weakness (gluteus medius/maximus)
- Foot pronation increasing knee valgus

**Clinical Tests:**
- Patellar apprehension test
- Patellar grind test (Clarke's sign)
- J-sign during active knee extension
- Single leg squat (knee valgus)
- Step-down test (poor control)

#### 2. **Hip-Related Knee Pain (Referred Pain)**
**Probability**: HIGH (hip pathology commonly refers to knee)

**Key Features:**
- Hip pathology can refer pain to anterior thigh and knee
- L3 dermatome overlap (hip joint and anterior knee)
- Knee examination may be normal
- Hip ROM restrictions present
- Pain with hip-loading activities

**Mechanism:**
- Intra-articular hip pathology
- Hip joint capsule irritation
- L3 nerve root involvement

**Clinical Tests:**
- Hip ROM assessment (especially IR, flexion)
- FABER test
- FADIR test
- Log roll test
- Stinchfield test

#### 3. **Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI)**
**Probability**: MODERATE-HIGH (common in young, active adults)

**Key Features:**
- Anterior hip/groin pain
- C-sign (patient cups anterior hip with hand)
- Pain with hip flexion + internal rotation
- Stiffness after sitting ("sitting sign")
- Clicking or catching sensation
- Difficulty with deep squatting

**Types:**
- **Cam impingement**: Femoral head-neck junction abnormality
- **Pincer impingement**: Acetabular overcoverage
- **Mixed**: Both cam and pincer

**Clinical Tests:**
- FADIR test (high sensitivity)
- FABER test
- Scour test
- Hip internal rotation <20° (suggests FAI)

#### 4. **Iliotibial Band Syndrome (ITBS)**
**Probability**: MODERATE

**Key Features:**
- Lateral knee pain
- Pain at 30° knee flexion (impingement zone)
- Pain with stairs, running (especially downhill)
- Snapping sensation possible
- Tenderness over lateral femoral epicondyle

**Mechanism:**
- ITB friction over lateral femoral condyle
- Compression of underlying bursa
- Hip abductor weakness
- Excessive foot pronation

**Clinical Tests:**
- Noble compression test
- Ober's test (ITB tightness)
- Palpation tenderness at lateral epicondyle
- Single leg squat (hip drop, knee valgus)

#### 5. **Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS)**
**Probability**: MODERATE

**Key Features:**
- Lateral hip pain
- Pain lying on affected side
- Tenderness over greater trochanter
- May refer to lateral thigh/knee
- Pain with stairs (hip abductor loading)

**Mechanism:**
- Gluteus medius/minimus tendinopathy
- Trochanteric bursitis
- Hip abductor weakness

**Clinical Tests:**
- Palpation over greater trochanter
- Single leg stance (Trendelenburg)
- Resisted hip abduction
- FABER test

### Secondary Considerations (Lower Likelihood)

#### 6. **Hip Labral Tear**
**Probability**: LOW-MODERATE

**Key Features:**
- Deep hip/groin pain
- Clicking, catching, or locking
- Pain with pivoting or twisting
- May have mechanical symptoms
- Often associated with FAI

**Clinical Tests:**
- FADIR test
- Scour test
- Fitzgerald test
- May need MRI arthrogram for diagnosis

#### 7. **Inflammatory Arthropathy (Early)**
**Probability**: LOW (but important to rule out)

**Key Features:**
- Morning stiffness >30-60 minutes
- Improvement with movement
- Multiple joint involvement possible
- Systemic symptoms (fatigue, fever)
- Family history

**Types:**
- Reactive arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Inflammatory bowel disease-related

**Red Flags:**
- Prolonged morning stiffness
- Systemic symptoms
- Multiple joint involvement
- Eye inflammation (uveitis)
- Skin rash (psoriasis)

#### 8. **Pes Anserine Bursitis/Tendinopathy**
**Probability**: LOW-MODERATE

**Key Features:**
- Medial knee pain below joint line
- Tenderness at pes anserine insertion
- Pain with stairs (especially ascending)
- May have hip weakness contributing

**Clinical Tests:**
- Palpation tenderness medial knee
- Resisted knee flexion
- Hip strength assessment

## RED FLAGS ASSESSMENT (CRITICAL)

### Serious Pathology Screening

#### **IMMEDIATE REFERRAL Required:**

**Infection (Septic Arthritis):**
- ❌ Fever, chills, rigors
- ❌ Hot, swollen, red joint
- ❌ Severe pain at rest
- ❌ Recent infection or immunocompromise
- ❌ Unable to weight-bear
- **Action**: Emergency medical referral

**Malignancy:**
- ❌ Unexplained weight loss (>10 lbs in 6 months)
- ❌ Night pain (unrelieved by position change)
- ❌ Constant, progressive pain
- ❌ History of cancer
- ❌ Age >50 or <20 with bone pain
- **Action**: Urgent medical referral

**Fracture:**
- ❌ Significant trauma
- ❌ Inability to weight-bear (Ottawa rules)
- ❌ Deformity or severe swelling
- ❌ Point tenderness over bone
- **Action**: Imaging and orthopedic referral

**Vascular Compromise:**
- ❌ Severe, unrelenting pain
- ❌ 5 P's: Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis
- ❌ Cold extremity
- **Action**: Emergency referral

#### **URGENT REFERRAL Required:**

**Inflammatory Arthritis (Systemic):**
- ⚠️ Morning stiffness >1 hour
- ⚠️ Multiple joint involvement
- ⚠️ Systemic symptoms (fever, fatigue, rash)
- ⚠️ Eye inflammation (uveitis)
- ⚠️ Psoriasis or IBD
- **Action**: Rheumatology referral

**Neurological Compromise:**
- ⚠️ Progressive weakness
- ⚠️ Saddle anesthesia
- ⚠️ Bowel/bladder dysfunction
- ⚠️ Bilateral symptoms with back pain
- **Action**: Emergency/urgent medical referral

### Yellow Flags (Psychosocial - Address in Treatment)
- Fear-avoidance beliefs
- Catastrophizing
- Depression or anxiety
- Poor coping strategies
- Belief that pain equals damage
- **Action**: Pain neuroscience education, psychology referral if severe

## PHYSICAL EXAMINATION

### Observation

#### **Standing Assessment (Anterior, Posterior, Lateral)**
- Pelvic alignment (ASIS, PSIS levels)
- Leg length discrepancy (measure if suspected)
- Knee alignment (varus, valgus, recurvatum)
- Foot posture (pronation, supination)
- Muscle atrophy (quadriceps, gluteals)
- Swelling or effusion
- Skin changes

#### **Gait Analysis** (CRITICAL for stair pain)
- Antalgic gait (shortened stance phase)
- Trendelenburg gait (hip abductor weakness)
- Reduced knee flexion in swing
- Foot progression angle
- Cadence and stride length
- Use of assistive devices

#### **Stair Climbing Assessment** (KEY for this patient)
**Ascending:**
- Lead leg preference
- Hip and knee flexion range
- Trunk lean (compensation for weakness)
- Handrail use

**Descending:**
- Eccentric quadriceps control
- Knee alignment (valgus collapse?)
- Step-to vs. step-over-step pattern
- Pain location and timing

### Range of Motion

#### **Hip ROM (Bilateral Comparison)**
| Movement | Normal Range | Assessment |
|----------|--------------|------------|
| Flexion | 120° | Supine, knee flexed |
| Extension | 10-15° | Thomas test position |
| Abduction | 45° | Supine |
| Adduction | 20-30° | Supine |
| Internal Rotation | 30-40° | Seated or prone |
| External Rotation | 40-50° | Seated or prone |

**Note**: Pain, stiffness, end-feel, capsular pattern (flexion, IR, abduction most limited)

#### **Knee ROM (Bilateral Comparison)**
| Movement | Normal Range | Assessment |
|----------|--------------|------------|
| Flexion | 135-140° | Prone or supine |
| Extension | 0° (±5-10°) | Supine |

**Note**: Pain location, crepitus, end-feel, patellar tracking

### Muscle Length Testing

**Hip Flexors:**
- **Thomas Test**: Iliopsoas and rectus femoris tightness
- **Modified Thomas**: Differentiate iliopsoas vs. rectus femoris
- **Ely's Test**: Rectus femoris specific

**Hip Adductors:**
- Supine hip abduction with knee extended

**Hamstrings:**
- Straight leg raise (SLR) - normal >80°
- 90/90 test

**Iliotibial Band:**
- **Ober's Test**: ITB and TFL tightness
- **Modified Ober's**: More specific for ITB

**Ankle:**
- Dorsiflexion (knee extended vs. flexed) - normal >10cm in lunge test

### Muscle Strength Testing (0-5 Scale)

#### **Hip Musculature** (CRITICAL for stair climbing)
| Muscle Group | Test Position | Normal Strength |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Hip Flexion (Iliopsoas) | Supine, resist flexion | 5/5 |
| Hip Extension (Glut Max) | Prone, resist extension | 5/5 |
| Hip Abduction (Glut Med) | Side-lying, resist abduction | 5/5 |
| Hip Adduction | Supine, resist adduction | 5/5 |
| Hip IR | Seated, resist IR | 5/5 |
| Hip ER | Seated, resist ER | 5/5 |

#### **Knee Musculature**
| Muscle Group | Test Position | Normal Strength |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Knee Extension (Quads) | Seated, resist extension | 5/5 |
| Knee Flexion (Hams) | Prone, resist flexion | 5/5 |

#### **Functional Strength Tests** (KEY)
- **Single Leg Squat**: Depth, control, knee alignment
- **Single Leg Stance**: 30 seconds, Trendelenburg sign
- **Step-Down Test**: Control, knee valgus, repetitions

### Special Tests

#### **Hip-Specific Tests**

| Test | Procedure | Positive Finding | Indicates |
|------|-----------|------------------|-----------|
| **FABER** | Hip Flex-ABduct-ER, apply overpressure | Anterior hip/groin pain | FAI, labral tear, hip OA |
| **FADIR** | Hip Flex-ADduct-IR | Anterior hip/groin pain | FAI, labral tear (78% sensitive) |
| **Scour** | Hip flexed 90°, compress + rotate | Pain or catching | Labral tear, FAI, OA |
| **Log Roll** | Supine, passive hip IR/ER | Pain with minimal movement | Intra-articular pathology |
| **Trendelenburg** | Single leg stance 30 sec | Contralateral pelvis drop | Hip abductor weakness |
| **Stinchfield** | Supine, resist hip flexion at 30° | Anterior hip pain | Intra-articular pathology |

#### **Knee-Specific Tests**

| Test | Procedure | Positive Finding | Indicates |
|------|-----------|------------------|-----------|
| **Patellar Apprehension** | Lateral patellar glide, knee flexed | Apprehension, pain | Patellar instability |
| **Patellar Grind (Clarke's)** | Compress patella, contract quads | Pain, crepitus | PFPS, chondromalacia |
| **J-Sign** | Observe patellar tracking during extension | Lateral deviation at terminal extension | Patellar maltracking |
| **Noble Compression** | Compress ITB at 30° flexion | Lateral knee pain | ITBS |
| **Ober's Test** | Side-lying, hip abduction | Leg remains abducted | ITB/TFL tightness |
| **McMurray's** | Rotation with flexion/extension | Click, pain | Meniscal tear |

## BIOMECHANICAL ASSESSMENT

### Kinetic Chain Analysis

#### **Foot and Ankle**
- Foot posture (pronation increases knee valgus)
- Ankle dorsiflexion (limited DF increases knee stress)
- Weight-bearing lunge test (>10cm normal)

#### **Knee Alignment**
- Q-angle (normal 13-18°, >20° increases PFP risk)
- Dynamic knee valgus during squat, step-down
- Patellar tracking (J-sign, lateral glide)

#### **Hip Control**
- Gluteal strength (abduction, extension)
- Trendelenburg sign (hip abductor weakness)
- Hip IR/ER control (excessive IR increases knee valgus)

#### **Pelvis and Core**
- Pelvic tilt (anterior tilt increases hip flexor tightness)
- Core stability (ability to maintain neutral spine)

### Stair Biomechanics (CRITICAL for this case)

#### **Ascending Stairs**
- Hip: 67° flexion, concentric hip extensors
- Knee: 83° flexion, concentric quadriceps
- Ankle: 23° dorsiflexion

**Common Compensations:**
- Lateral trunk lean (hip abductor weakness)
- Reduced knee flexion (quadriceps weakness/pain)
- Pulling with arms (lower extremity weakness)

#### **Descending Stairs**
- Hip: Eccentric hip extensors
- Knee: 90° flexion, eccentric quadriceps (HIGHEST patellofemoral stress)
- Ankle: Controlled dorsiflexion

**Common Compensations:**
- Slow, cautious descent (pain or weakness)
- Lateral shift (unloading painful side)
- Step-to pattern (not step-over-step)

## CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

### Myofascial Factors

**Muscle Tightness:**
- Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris)
- Hamstrings
- ITB/TFL
- Gastrocnemius/soleus

**Muscle Weakness:**
- Gluteus maximus (hip extension)
- Gluteus medius/minimus (hip abduction)
- Quadriceps (especially VMO)
- Hip external rotators

**Trigger Points:**
- Quadriceps → anterior knee pain
- Gluteals → hip and lateral thigh pain
- TFL/ITB → lateral knee pain

### Articular Factors

**Joint Mobility:**
- Hip capsular restriction (IR, flexion)
- Patellar mobility restrictions
- Ankle dorsiflexion restriction

**Joint Alignment:**
- Hip dysplasia or FAI morphology
- Patellar maltracking
- Knee varus/valgus
- Foot pronation

### Neuromuscular Factors

**Motor Control Deficits:**
- Delayed VMO activation
- Gluteal amnesia
- Quadriceps inhibition
- Poor proprioception

**Movement Pattern Dysfunction:**
- Quadriceps dominance
- Compensatory strategies
- Kinetic chain breakdown

### Biomechanical Factors

**Structural:**
- Leg length discrepancy
- Femoral anteversion
- Q-angle abnormalities
- Foot structure

**Load Management:**
- Training errors (too much, too soon)
- Overuse without recovery
- Sudden activity changes

## EVIDENCE-BASED TREATMENT PLAN

### Phase 1: Pain and Inflammation Management (Weeks 1-2)

#### **Goals:**
- Reduce pain from 8/10 to 4-5/10
- Improve stair climbing tolerance
- Restore normal gait pattern
- Patient education on condition

#### **Manual Therapy Interventions**

**1. Mulligan Mobilization with Movement (MWM)**
*Reference: [Mulligan Concept](../manual-therapy/mulligan-concept.md)*

**Hip MWM:**
- Hip flexion MWM with posterior glide
- Hip IR MWM with lateral glide
- Weight-bearing hip MWM for functional activities
- **Dosage**: 3 sets × 10 reps, 2-3x/week
- **Evidence**: Strong for immediate pain relief

**Knee MWM:**
- Patellofemoral medial glide MWM
- Tibiofemoral IR MWM
- Weight-bearing knee MWM for stairs
- **Dosage**: 3 sets × 10 reps, 2-3x/week
- **Rule**: MUST be pain-free during application

**2. Joint Mobilizations (Maitland)**

**Hip:**
- Grade III-IV caudal glide (general stiffness)
- Grade III-IV posterior glide (flexion restriction)
- Grade III-IV lateral glide (IR restriction)
- **Dosage**: 3 × 30-60 seconds, 2-3x/week

**Knee:**
- Patellofemoral mobilizations (all directions)
- Tibiofemoral anterior/posterior glides
- **Dosage**: 3 × 30-60 seconds, 2-3x/week

**3. Myofascial Release**

**Hip Region:**
- Iliopsoas release (90-120 sec)
- TFL/ITB release (2-3 min)
- Gluteal release (90-120 sec per point)
- Piriformis release (90-120 sec)

**Knee Region:**
- Quadriceps release (2-3 min)
- Hamstring release (90-120 sec per point)
- Gastrocnemius release (2-3 min)

**Self-Care:**
- Foam rolling ITB, quadriceps, hamstrings
- Ball release for gluteals, hip flexors
- **Frequency**: Daily, 10-15 minutes

**4. Muscle Energy Technique (MET)**

- Hip flexor MET (3-5 reps)
- Hamstring MET (3-5 reps)
- ITB/TFL MET (3-5 reps)
- **Dosage**: 2-3x/week in clinic, daily at home

#### **Therapeutic Exercise - Phase 1**

**Pain-Free Range of Motion:**
- Supine hip flexion/extension
- Supine knee flexion/extension
- Ankle pumps
- **Dosage**: 2 × 15 reps, 3x/day

**Gentle Strengthening:**
- Isometric quadriceps sets
- Isometric gluteal sets
- Isometric hip abduction (side-lying)
- **Dosage**: 10-second holds, 2 × 10 reps, 2x/day

**Stretching:**
- Hip flexor stretch (30 sec × 3)
- Hamstring stretch (30 sec × 3)
- ITB stretch (30 sec × 3)
- Calf stretch (30 sec × 3)
- **Frequency**: 2-3x/day

#### **Pain Neuroscience Education**
*Reference: [Pain Neuroscience Education](../pain-science/pain-neuroscience-education.md)*

**Session 1: Pain Basics (30 min)**
- "Pain does not equal damage"
- Difference between hurt and harm
- Acute vs. chronic pain
- Role of the nervous system
- **Goal**: Reduce fear-avoidance, increase understanding

**Key Messages:**
- Stair pain is common and treatable
- Movement is safe and beneficial
- Pain with activity doesn't mean you're causing damage
- Gradual return to activities is the goal

**Educational Materials:**
- Handouts on pain science
- Diagrams of knee/hip anatomy
- Activity modification guidelines

#### **Activity Modification**

**Stair Climbing:**
- Use handrail for support
- Lead with stronger leg ascending
- Lead with weaker leg descending
- Take one step at a time initially
- Reduce speed, increase control

**Avoid/Modify:**
- Deep squatting
- Prolonged sitting (>30 min without movement)
- Running, jumping (temporarily)
- High-impact activities

**Encourage:**
- Walking (flat surfaces, pain-free distance)
- Swimming or water exercises
- Cycling (if pain-free)
- Upper body exercises

#### **Load Management**

**Principles:**
- Stay within pain tolerance (0-3/10 acceptable)
- Gradual progression (10% rule)
- Adequate rest between sessions
- Monitor pain response (24-48 hours)

**Activity Pacing:**
- Break activities into smaller chunks
- Alternate between activities
- Schedule rest periods
- Avoid "boom-bust" cycle

### Phase 2: Strength and Motor Control (Weeks 3-6)

#### **Goals:**
- Further reduce pain to 2-3/10
- Restore normal ROM
- Improve strength (4/5 or better)
- Improve movement quality
- Return to stairs without pain

#### **Manual Therapy - Progression**

**Continue:**
- MWM techniques as needed
- Joint mobilizations (reduce frequency)
- Myofascial release (self-management focus)

**Add:**
- PNF techniques for strengthening
- Advanced mobilizations as needed

#### **Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation (PNF)**
*Reference: [PNF](../neuromuscular-movement/proprioceptive-neuromuscular-facilitation.md)*

**Lower Extremity Patterns:**
- D1 flexion/extension (hip, knee)
- D2 flexion/extension (hip, knee)
- **Techniques**: Repeated contractions, timing for emphasis
- **Dosage**: 5-10 reps per pattern, 2-3x/week

**Benefits:**
- Facilitates weak muscles through irradiation
- Improves coordination
- Enhances functional movement patterns

#### **Therapeutic Exercise - Phase 2**

**Strengthening Progression:**

**Hip Strengthening:**
1. **Gluteus Maximus:**
   - Bridges (2 × 15)
   - Single leg bridges (2 × 10)
   - Prone hip extension (2 × 15)
   - Quadruped hip extension (2 × 15)

2. **Gluteus Medius:**
   - Side-lying hip abduction (2 × 15)
   - Clamshells (2 × 15)
   - Side plank with hip abduction (2 × 10)
   - Monster walks with resistance band (2 × 20 steps)

3. **Hip Flexors (controlled):**
   - Standing hip flexion with resistance (2 × 15)
   - Marching in place (2 × 30 sec)

**Knee Strengthening:**
1. **Quadriceps:**
   - Straight leg raises (2 × 15)
   - Short arc quads (2 × 15)
   - Terminal knee extension with band (2 × 15)
   - Wall sits (3 × 30-60 sec)

2. **Hamstrings:**
   - Prone knee curls (2 × 15)
   - Standing knee curls with band (2 × 15)
   - Stability ball hamstring curls (2 × 10)

**Motor Control Training:**

1. **Single Leg Balance:**
   - Eyes open (30 sec × 3)
   - Eyes closed (30 sec × 3)
   - On unstable surface (30 sec × 3)
   - With perturbations (30 sec × 3)

2. **Squat Progression:**
   - Mini squats (0-45°) (2 × 15)
   - Partial squats (0-60°) (2 × 15)
   - Full squats (0-90°) (2 × 10)
   - **Focus**: Knee alignment, hip hinge, control

3. **Single Leg Squat:**
   - Assisted (holding support) (2 × 10)
   - Unassisted (2 × 10)
   - **Focus**: No knee valgus, pelvic control

4. **Step Training:**
   - Step-ups (4-6 inch step) (2 × 10)
   - Step-downs (4-6 inch step) (2 × 10)
   - Lateral step-ups (2 × 10)
   - **Progress**: Increase height, add resistance

5. **Lunge Progression:**
   - Static lunges (2 × 10 each leg)
   - Forward lunges (2 × 10 each leg)
   - Reverse lunges (2 × 10 each leg)
   - Walking lunges (2 × 20 steps)

**Frequency:** 3-4x/week, alternate days

#### **Functional Training**

**Stair Training (Gradual Progression):**
1. Single step, step-to pattern
2. Single step, step-over-step pattern
3. Multiple steps, handrail support
4. Multiple steps, no handrail
5. Increase speed
6. Add resistance (weighted vest, backpack)

**Gait Training:**
- Treadmill walking (flat, then incline)
- Outdoor walking (varied terrain)
- Increase distance and speed gradually

### Phase 3: Return to Function (Weeks 7-12)

#### **Goals:**
- Pain 0-2/10 with all activities
- Full ROM
- Strength 5/5
- Normal movement patterns
- Return to all desired activities

#### **Advanced Strengthening**

**Plyometric Training (if appropriate):**
- Double leg hops (2 × 10)
- Single leg hops (2 × 10)
- Box jumps (2 × 10)
- Lateral hops (2 × 10)
- **Progress gradually, ensure good control**

**Sport-Specific Training:**
- Agility drills
- Cutting and pivoting
- Sport-specific movements
- Gradual return to sport

**Resistance Training:**
- Squats with weight (3 × 10)
- Deadlifts (3 × 10)
- Lunges with weight (3 × 10)
- Leg press (3 × 10)

#### **Maintenance Program**

**Ongoing Exercises (2-3x/week):**
- Hip strengthening (glutes)
- Knee strengthening (quads)
- Balance and proprioception
- Flexibility maintenance

**Self-Management:**
- Foam rolling routine
- Stretching program
- Activity monitoring
- Load management principles
