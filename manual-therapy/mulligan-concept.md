# Mulligan Concept and Mobilization with Movement (MWM)

## Overview

The Mulligan Concept, developed by New Zealand physiotherapist Brian Mulligan, is a manual therapy approach that combines sustained accessory mobilization applied by a therapist with active physiological movement performed by the patient. The hallmark technique is Mobilization with Movement (MWM).

## Core Principles

### 1. **Positional Fault Theory**
- Joint dysfunction results from minor positional faults in joint alignment
- These faults are too small to be detected on imaging but cause pain and movement restriction
- Manual correction of the positional fault restores pain-free movement

### 2. **Pain-Free Movement**
- All techniques must be completely pain-free during application
- If pain occurs, the technique is either incorrect or inappropriate
- "No pain during treatment" is a fundamental rule

### 3. **Immediate Results**
- Techniques should produce immediate improvement in pain and/or range of motion
- If no immediate change occurs, reassess technique application or consider alternative approaches

## Key Techniques

### Mobilization with Movement (MWM)
- **Application**: Sustained accessory glide applied by therapist while patient performs active movement
- **Common Applications**:
  - Spinal MWMs (SNAGs - Sustained Natural Apophyseal Glides)
  - Peripheral joint MWMs (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle)
  - Weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing variations

### Natural Apophyseal Glides (NAGs)
- **Application**: Oscillatory mobilizations applied to cervical and thoracic spine
- **Technique**: Therapist applies gliding force parallel to facet joint plane
- **Use**: Primarily for spinal stiffness and pain

### Sustained Natural Apophyseal Glides (SNAGs)
- **Application**: Sustained glide during active movement
- **Technique**: Therapist maintains glide while patient moves into previously restricted/painful range
- **Use**: Spinal pain with movement restriction

### Spinal Mobilization with Leg Movement (SMWLM)
- **Application**: Spinal mobilization combined with straight leg raise
- **Use**: Nerve-related leg pain, radiculopathy

### Taping
- **Purpose**: Maintain correction achieved with manual techniques
- **Application**: Applied to replicate the manual glide direction
- **Duration**: Can be worn between treatment sessions

## Clinical Indications

### Spinal Conditions
- Cervical pain and stiffness
- Thoracic pain and dysfunction
- Lumbar pain and movement restriction
- Facet joint dysfunction
- Nerve-related pain (with appropriate modifications)

### Peripheral Joint Conditions
- Lateral epicondylalgia (tennis elbow)
- Shoulder impingement and restriction
- Ankle sprains and chronic instability
- Knee pain and patellofemoral dysfunction
- Wrist and hand joint restrictions

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications
- Malignancy affecting the treatment area
- Active infection or inflammation
- Fractures or bone instability
- Cauda equina syndrome
- Vertebrobasilar insufficiency (for cervical techniques)

### Relative Contraindications
- Acute inflammatory arthritis
- Severe osteoporosis
- Hypermobility syndromes
- Neurological signs requiring medical investigation
- Patient unable to relax or provide accurate feedback

## Evidence Base

### Research Support
- **Lateral Epicondylalgia**: Strong evidence for immediate pain relief and functional improvement
- **Ankle Sprains**: Moderate evidence for improved dorsiflexion and reduced pain
- **Shoulder Dysfunction**: Emerging evidence for rotator cuff-related pain and restricted movement
- **Spinal Pain**: Mixed evidence; some studies show short-term benefits for cervical and lumbar pain

### Systematic Reviews
- Multiple systematic reviews support MWM for various musculoskeletal conditions
- Evidence strongest for peripheral joint applications
- Need for more high-quality RCTs for spinal applications

## Clinical Application Guidelines

### Assessment
1. Identify painful or restricted movement
2. Determine appropriate accessory glide direction
3. Apply sustained glide while patient performs active movement
4. Assess for immediate pain relief and improved range

### Treatment Progression
1. **Initial Session**: Establish pain-free technique, 3 sets of 10 repetitions
2. **Subsequent Sessions**: Progress load, range, and functional integration
3. **Home Program**: Self-mobilization techniques and taping as appropriate
4. **Functional Integration**: Incorporate into functional activities and exercises

### Dosage
- **Repetitions**: 3 sets of 10 repetitions per session
- **Frequency**: 2-3 times per week initially
- **Duration**: Typically 4-6 sessions, reassess if no progress after 3 sessions

## Integration with Other Approaches

### Complementary Techniques
- **Exercise Therapy**: MWM prepares joints for strengthening and functional exercises
- **Motor Control Training**: Improved joint mechanics enhance motor control retraining
- **Pain Neuroscience Education**: Addresses pain beliefs while providing mechanical solutions
- **Taping**: Maintains correction between sessions

### Combined Approaches
- Often integrated with McKenzie Method for spinal conditions
- Combines well with therapeutic exercise prescription
- Can precede strengthening programs to optimize joint mechanics

## Training and Certification

### Official Training
- **Mulligan Concept Teachers Association (MCTA)**: International organization
- **Course Levels**:
  - Level 1: Upper and Lower Quadrant (4 days each)
  - Level 2: Advanced applications
  - Level 3: Spinal applications
- **Certification**: Available after completing required courses and examination

### Prerequisites
- Licensed physical therapist or equivalent healthcare professional
- Strong foundation in musculoskeletal assessment and manual therapy

## Clinical Pearls

- **Always pain-free**: If technique causes pain, stop and reassess
- **Immediate effect**: Should see improvement within first application
- **Patient active**: Patient must actively move while therapist maintains glide
- **Functional integration**: Progress to weight-bearing and functional positions
- **Taping reinforcement**: Use tape to maintain gains between sessions
- **Reassess frequently**: If no progress after 2-3 sessions, consider alternative approach
